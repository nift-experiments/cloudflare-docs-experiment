

Steps vary depending on your desktop environment.

<details class="nb-details"><summary>GNOME (Ubuntu, Fedora)</summary><div class="nb-details-body">

<ol>
<li>Open <strong>Settings</strong> &gt; <strong>Network</strong>.</li>
<li>Select the gear icon next to your active connection.</li>
<li>Select the <strong>Proxy</strong> tab.</li>
<li>Set the method to <strong>Automatic</strong>.</li>
<li>In the <strong>Configuration URL</strong> field, enter your PAC file URL.</li>
</ol>

</div></details>

<details class="nb-details"><summary>KDE Plasma</summary><div class="nb-details-body">

<ol>
<li>Open <strong>System Settings</strong> &gt; <strong>Network Settings</strong> &gt; <strong>Proxy</strong>.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Use proxy auto configuration URL</strong>.</li>
<li>In the URL field, enter your PAC file URL.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Apply</strong>.</li>
</ol>

</div></details>

<aside class="nb-aside note">


<p>Most Linux command-line tools (such as <code>curl</code> and <code>wget</code>) do not natively support PAC files. The system proxy settings apply to GUI browsers only. For command-line tools, configure the <code>http_proxy</code> and <code>https_proxy</code> environment variables with your proxy endpoint address directly.</p>


</aside>
