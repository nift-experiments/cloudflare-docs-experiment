

Consider that the same Cloudflare account has two different zones, `example.com` and `example.net`.

<div class="nb-example"><h3 class="nb-component-title" id="body-7591-example">Example</h3>

<p>DNS management for <strong>example.com</strong>:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Type</th>
<th>Name</th>
<th>Content</th>
<th>Proxy status</th>
<th>TTL</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>CNAME</td>
<td><code>example.com</code></td>
<td><code>origin.example.net</code></td>
<td>DNS only</td>
<td>Auto</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</div>

<div class="nb-example"><h3 class="nb-component-title" id="body-7591-example-1">Example</h3>

<p>DNS management for <strong>example.net</strong>:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Type</th>
<th>Name</th>
<th>Content</th>
<th>Proxy status</th>
<th>TTL</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>CNAME</td>
<td><code>origin.example.net</code></td>
<td><code>&lt;origin&gt;</code></td>
<td>Proxied</td>
<td>Auto</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</div>

In this example, all traffic intended for `example.com` will be treated as **Proxied**.

<aside class="nb-aside note">


<p>CNAME to a different Cloudflare account is prohibited and will result in a <a href="/support/troubleshooting/http-status-codes/cloudflare-1xxx-errors/error-1014/">Error 1014 (CNAME Cross-User Banned)</a></p>


</aside>
