<p>Cloudflare Tunnel allows you to publish local applications to the Internet via a public hostname. For example, you can <a href="/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/get-started/create-remote-tunnel/#2a-publish-an-application">add a published application route</a> that points <code>docs.example.com</code> to <code>https://localhost:8080</code>. Anyone can now view your application by going to <code>docs.example.com</code> in their web browser.</p>
<p>Cloudflare can route traffic down your Cloudflare Tunnel using a <a href="/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/routing-to-tunnel/dns/">DNS record</a> or <a href="/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/routing-to-tunnel/public-load-balancers/">Cloudflare Load Balancer</a>. You can configure either option from the Cloudflare dashboard by pointing a DNS <code>CNAME</code> record or a load balancer pool to your Cloudflare Tunnel subdomain (<code>&lt;UUID&gt;.cfargotunnel.com</code>). You can also associate these records with your tunnel from <code>cloudflared</code> directly.</p>
<p>For TCP or UDP applications, Cloudflare can also route traffic through a <a href="/spectrum/get-started/#create-a-spectrum-application-using-a-virtual-network-origin">Spectrum application</a> attached to a <a href="/cloudflare-one/networks/virtual-networks/">virtual network</a>.</p>
