<p>Quarterly DDoS threat reports provide a comprehensive overview of <span class="nb-glossary-tooltip" title="distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attack">DDoS attack</span> insights and trends over a three-month period.</p>
<p>Thanks to our vast network, Cloudflare provides insights on the evolving threat landscape, including variations in attack sizes, techniques, top source countries, top targeted countries and targeted industries. Each report presents a global outlook, dives into significant attacks and campaigns, and explores shifts in DDoS tactics, offering a blend of data analysis and insights to better understand the cyber threat environment.</p>
<p>Find the latest quarterly DDoS threat reports in the <a href="https://radar.cloudflare.com/reports"><strong>Reports</strong></a> section of Cloudflare Radar.</p>
<hr />
<h2 id="methodologies">Methodologies</h2>
<h3 id="how-we-count-the-number-of-ddos-attacks">How we count the number of DDoS attacks</h3>
<p>Cloudflare's main DDoS system, the <a href="/ddos-protection/managed-rulesets/">DDoS Managed Ruleset</a>, generates real-time fingerprints for DDoS attacks that it automatically detects and mitigates. While there may be multiple fingerprints generated for a single DDoS attack, or attack campaign, we count unique fingerprints that resulted in mitigation to get an understanding of the number of DDoS attacks for a given period of time. While in some cases, we can see an 'explosion' of fingerprints due to randomized DDoS attacks, for the most part, this figure gives us a reliable indicator to track over time.</p>
<p>Currently, the number of DDoS attacks does not take into consideration the <a href="/ddos-protection/advanced-ddos-systems/overview/advanced-tcp-protection/">Advanced TCP Protection system</a> or the <a href="/ddos-protection/advanced-ddos-systems/overview/advanced-dns-protection/">Advanced DNS Protection system</a>. We also don't take into account any mitigations by customer-created rules or configuration.</p>
<h3 id="how-we-calculate-ransom-ddos-attack-insights">How we calculate ransom DDoS attack insights</h3>
<p>Cloudflare’s systems constantly analyze traffic and automatically apply mitigation when DDoS attacks are detected. Each attacked customer is prompted with an automated survey to help Cloudflare better understand the nature of the attack and the success of the mitigation. For over two years, Cloudflare has been surveying attacked customers. One of the questions in the survey asks the respondents if they received a threat or a ransom note. Over the past few years, on average, Cloudflare has been collecting around 200 responses per quarter. The responses of this survey are used to calculate the percentage of ransom DDoS attacks.</p>
<h3 id="how-we-calculate-geographical-and-industry-insights">How we calculate geographical and industry insights</h3>
<h4 id="source-country">Source country</h4>
<p>At the application layer, Cloudflare uses the attacking IP addresses to understand the origin country of the attacks. That is because at that layer, IP addresses cannot be spoofed <sup><a href="#footnote-1">1</a></sup> (or modified). However, at the network layer, source IP addresses can be spoofed. So, instead of relying on IP addresses to understand the source, Cloudflare uses the location of our data centers where the attack packets were ingested. It is possible to obtain geographical accuracy due to Cloudflare's large global coverage in over 300 locations around the world.</p>
<h4 id="target-country">Target country</h4>
<p>For both application-layer and network-layer DDoS attacks, attacks and traffic are grouped by customers’ billing country. This allows Cloudflare to understand which countries are subject to more attacks.</p>
<h4 id="target-industry">Target industry</h4>
<p>For both application-layer and network-layer DDoS attacks, attacks and traffic are grouped by customers’ industry according to the internal customer relations management system. This allows Cloudflare to understand which industries are subject to more attacks.</p>
<h4 id="total-volume-versus-percentage">Total volume versus percentage</h4>
<p>For both source and target insights, Cloudflare looks at the total volume of attack traffic compared to all traffic as one data point. Additionally, Cloudflare also takes into account the percentage of attack traffic towards or from a specific country, to a specific country or to a specific industry. This gives us an &quot;attack activity rate&quot; for a given country/industry which is normalized by their total traffic levels. This helps us remove biases of a country or industry that normally receives a lot of traffic and therefore, a lot of attack traffic as well.</p>
<h4 id="ranking">Ranking</h4>
<p>For source, target, and industry insights, Cloudflare may calculate a &quot;Rank&quot; for each dimension. The calculation takes into consideration HTTP DDoS attacks, network-layer DDoS attacks, and the total volume and the percentage of DDoS attack traffic out of the total traffic for each attack type. The Ranking system lets Cloudflare provide a simple single score that means how much a certain industry is being attacked, for example. In the graphs, the Rank values are displayed in an inverted way (a longer bar in the chart means a higher rank and more attacks).</p>
<h3 id="how-we-calculate-attack-characteristics">How we calculate attack characteristics</h3>
<p>To calculate the attack size, duration, attack vectors, and emerging threats, Cloudflare buckets attacks and then provides the share of each bucket out of the total amount for each dimension.</p>
<p>However, in the <strong>Network layer attack distribution</strong> graph of the <a href="https://radar.cloudflare.com/security-and-attacks"><strong>Security &amp; Attacks</strong></a> Radar page these trends are calculated by number of bytes instead. Since attacks may vary greatly in number of bytes from one another, this could lead to trends differing between the quarterly reports and the graph displayed in Cloudflare Radar.</p>
<hr />
<h2 id="final-remarks">Final remarks</h2>
<h3 id="countries-as-source-or-target-of-attacks">Countries as source or target of attacks</h3>
<p>When Cloudflare describes &quot;top countries&quot; as the source or target of attacks, it does not necessarily mean that a certain country was attacked as a country, but rather that organizations that use that country as their billing country were targeted by attacks.</p>
<p>Similarly, &quot;attacks originating from a country&quot; does not mean that a given country launched the attacks, but rather that the attack was launched from IP addresses mapped to that country. Threat actors operate global botnets with nodes all over the world, and often also use <span class="nb-glossary-tooltip" title="Virtual Private Network (VPN)">VPNs (virtual private networks)</span> and proxies to obfuscate their true location. This means that the source country could indicate the presence of exit nodes or botnet nodes within that country.</p>
<h3 id="excluded-items-due-to-insufficient-data">Excluded items due to insufficient data</h3>
<p>The insights and trends presented in quarterly reports exclude certain countries and industries when there is not enough data to provide statistically meaningful insights.</p>
<h3 id="map-chart-coloring">Map chart coloring</h3>
<p>In the map charts, the countries and regions are colored using a diverging scale, ranging from white (minimum value) to red (maximum value). These vary according to the selected world continent group.</p>
