<p>Dashes look like hyphens, but are wider. An em dash is the widest type of dash. Dashes have a different purpose than hyphens. We use two types of dashes:</p>
<h2 id="hyphen">Hyphen (-)</h2>
<p><strong>Hyphenate compound modifiers that are placed before the noun.</strong> Compound modifiers that come before the noun require a hyphen to distinguish them from individual modifiers and nouns. Do not hyphenate compound modifiers that come after the noun. Hyphens are used in compound words and to express ranges.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>The most up-to-date software can be found here.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>It takes 24-48 hours for the data to upload.</p>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="em-dash">Em dash (—)</h2>
<p><strong>Use em dashes to break up thoughts within a single sentence.</strong> Em dashes (—) or double dashes (--) with spaces around them may be used to break up thoughts within a sentence. Single dashes (-), or hyphens, are commonly used to write compound words/modifiers and should never be used to break up thoughts in this way.</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Shortcut for Mac users: Hold down Shift and Option keys, then press the Minus key</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Shortcut for PC users: Hold down the Alt key and type 0151</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Dashes are used in pairs (in place of parenthesis) or alone (in place of a semicolon or colon).</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Cookies provide saved information about a user’s session to establish a “state” — in most cases, identity — as your browser makes a request.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Embed yourself in the team — the best results will come when trust is built.</p>
</li>
</ul>
