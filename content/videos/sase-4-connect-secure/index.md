The focus is often user to application,</p><p>where the goal is network micro-segmentation,</p><p>and a user can only access an application over a specific address and port.</p><p>But corporate networks exist to carry traffic in many other ways.</p><p>Let's take, for example, a retail coffee company with many coffee</p><p>shops each providing customers free access to the Internet with their guest Wi-Fi,</p><p>but also connecting employees to internal applications.</p><p>Each shop also houses point of sale devices, security cameras,</p><p>and other network enabled equipment that need access to the Internet,</p><p>but also might require access to other private networks to back up data or be</p><p>monitored by internal tools.</p><p>IT staff also need to remotely access these devices from a corporate office network.</p><p>A lot of this traffic is private and should only remain on the corporate network.</p><p>This is where Cloudflare's Connectivity Cloud really comes into its own.</p><p>The ability to mesh together different networks,</p><p>applications and users no matter where they are.</p><p>Let's dive deeper into our coffee company example.</p><p>Right. First, they have their main headquarters in Seattle.</p><p>Most HQ employees live locally and about half travel into the office,</p><p>with the other half working remote at home.</p><p>Second, they have around 40 coffee shops down the west coast of America,</p><p>each with a few employees in each.</p><p>And then they have an internal company wiki, which is running in a virtual environment in</p><p>Amazon Web Services with its own virtual private network.</p><p>And then finally, the security cameras at all their coffee shops need to back up data to a</p><p>central service that you've got running on servers that you run and host in a rack in a</p><p>data center in San Jose.</p><p>You see how these network locations are all quite different.</p><p>Cloudflare has a variety of ways all these networks can be connected together.</p><p>Let's start by connecting the headquarters network in Seattle.</p><p>We can use something called Magic WAN, which is our service that creates IPsec</p><p>tunnels from the headquarters office back to the Cloudflare network and assign a private</p><p>network range to it.
